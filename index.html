Stationery Inventory

For stationery lovers who keep buying notebooks they already own: see everything you have, where it lives, and what's still waiting to be used.

NEW

01 The problem

I've bought many notebooks I didn't need, because I thought I didn't have one. In a shop or shopping online, I bought another spiral-bound A5 graph notebook, and one was already waiting for me on my bookshelf, where I keep all my empty notebooks.

So this isn't an app for remembering where I put things. It's for knowing what I already have, so I don't buy more of the same, and for reaching for the brand-new notebook that's waiting for me.

02 What my users told me

I asked three people with a stationery stash about the last notebook or pen they bought, what's in their bag right now, and whether they've ever tried to keep track of what they own.

Isha

Places big online stationery orders, carries an everyday set for work, and keeps the rest on a bookshelf.

Sometimes I buy duplicate items, sometimes intentionally sometimes no. I like to have multiple things to stock up my supply.
I have a bookshelf with my stationery. I have been meaning to create an inventory list for everything.

What I learned: Not every duplicate is a mistake. Isha stocks up on purpose, so the app should show how many there are and where, instead of a warning.

Anisha

Collects cute notebooks, and carries one pen and one notebook day to day.

I collect cute notebooks and never use them. I definitely have at least 5 or 6 that I haven't touched from when I was younger.
But I won't do an inventory for everything that's in my house.

What I learned: This app probably isn't for people like Anisha, who wouldn't keep an inventory of everything at home. It's for people like Isha, who already mean to keep a list.

Kaitlyn

Tutors, keeps one spiral notebook as a catch-all, and writes only in pencil.

In college I was serial notebook buyer, and I would not finish them, and I would donate them when they were empty. So now I try not to buy them.
I wanted a spiral bound one because it gets annoying if it's not spiral bound.

What I learned: Kaitlyn stopped buying notebooks because they never got finished, but still has unused ones. Binding alone was reason enough to buy another.

03 How it works

Your stash lives in three places: your work backpack, your home desk and your bookshelf. Open one to see what's inside, like the inventory screen in a video game.

Try it: switch places, hover over an item or tap it, and move it somewhere else. The items are samples, plus my Mnemosyne.

  • Add an item by typing its name and details. It lands in your default place as NEW.
  • Inspect an item to see its details and your own ratings.
  • NEW means unused. Unused items carry a NEW tag and show how long they've been waiting.
  • "Do I already have this?" In a shop, pick the type and its details, and see how many you already own and where each one is. No warning, because sometimes stocking up is the point.
  • "Move to…" sends an item to another place, so the bag and the shelf match real life.

Version one covers four kinds of stationery. The details are what a duplicate check matches on; the ratings are your own.

04 My focus: Design

The whole app will work end to end: people sign up and log their real stash. The part I'm going deep on is design, because I'm focusing on the user experience and the graphics.

Visual design

An illustrated bag, desk and bookshelf, item icons coloured like the real thing, and one cozy video-game style.

Flows

Adding an item, checking for a duplicate in a shop, and moving an item between places.

States

An empty bag, a NEW item waiting to be used, "2 like this: 1 on your bookshelf, 1 in your bag," and "nothing like this yet."

Accessibility

It works by keyboard, respects reduced motion, and screen readers read out each item's details.

Riskiest part first: the animations, because they're my focus and the approach isn't proven yet. I'll build one small animation during v1 week.

06 Stack, MCP servers and CLIs

PieceWhy it fits
Next.js + TailwindI've built my last few assignments on it, so setup is quick and the time goes into design.
ClerkReal sign-ups, so Isha and I each get our own account.
SupabaseStores everyone's items and places. Row-level security keeps each stash private.
MotionAnimates the bag opening and items moving between places, and turns animation off for anyone who has asked their device for reduced motion.
VercelHosting for the app and for this page.

MCP servers: the Supabase MCP server creates the tables and the row-level security rules, and Claude's built-in browser checks every screen and animation at phone and laptop widths.

CLIs: vercel to deploy, gh and git for the repo, npm for packages, and Playwright for a privacy test that proves Isha can't see my stash.

Free tiers: every service stays on its free tier, with usage limits and alerts turned on wherever they're offered.
